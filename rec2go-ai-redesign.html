Overview

Rec2Go is the Rutgers Recreation app. For my Interaction Design Studio final project I'm redesigning it in two parts. Part 1 asks AI prototyping tools to design the app and then critiques the result: strengths, weaknesses, what's missing, and where human judgment still matters. Part 2 turns that critique into a full human-centered redesign, with an interactive high-fidelity Figma prototype and usability testing.

The final piece will be documented the way PeerConnect is: research, process, decisions and a clickable prototype.

The Problem

I started with an audit of the current app, screen by screen. What stood out:

  • No hierarchy: all 19 home tiles carry the same weight, so Member ID, the thing you need at the door, looks as important as Social Media
  • Organized around the department, not the student: schedules are split into separate tiles by campus, so a simple question like “where can I play basketball right now?” takes several taps
  • Many tiles leave the app: they open the website in a browser, behind a pop-up with only a Continue button, in a different visual style each time
  • Static information: schedules don't show what's open now, and there are dead ends, like a tile for a service that has been discontinued
  • Empty states and promo banners: blank screens don't explain themselves, and a rotating promo banner appears on almost every screen

Working problem statement: students open Rec2Go to do a few quick things (get in, see what's open, join a class), but the app sends them out to the website for most tasks and makes them piece together information across campus-specific lists. Interviews will confirm which tasks matter most.

The Plan

Part 1: AI wireframing and critique

  • Prompt AI tools twice: a vague prompt and a detailed one with the real constraints (four campuses, ID check-in, class registration, content that lives on the website)
  • Compare what they generate with the real app, and annotate strengths, weaknesses and what's missing
  • Write an honest reflection on where AI helps and where human expertise stays critical

Part 2: human-centered redesign

  • Interview students about what they actually open Rec2Go for
  • Narrow to one or two core flows, such as getting in fast and finding and booking a class
  • Build an interactive high-fidelity prototype in Figma and usability-test it, benchmarking taps and screens before and after
